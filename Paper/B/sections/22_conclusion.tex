\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This article characterised a from-scratch reproduction of windowed phase
estimation and then evaluated a fix for what the characterisation found.

The characterisation established that varying any single parameter in
isolation produces no failures at all, and that starving several classical
budgets together fails 47.9\% of the time on an instance the same pipeline
solves trivially when any one budget is relaxed. A counterfactual ladder
applied to all 94 observed failures attributed 72\% of them to classical
budget starvation rather than to noise, corruption, or any defect in the
reconstruction logic. That attribution is what selects the classical
reconstruction stage as the place to intervene.

The evaluation found that the published pipeline succeeds on 52.1\% of the
canonical grid and the full adaptive configuration on 100\%, reaching
97.9--100\% across three pre-registered held-out instances. Matched-resource
controls qualify this and are reported as a primary finding: given each
module's own realised budget spread uniformly, no individual module is
distinguishable from its control. The evidence therefore supports automatic,
instance-adaptive selection of the reconstruction budget --- removing the need
to choose it correctly in advance --- rather than a demonstrated superiority
of confidence-guided placement over an equivalent uniform allocation.

Two limits carry through. All validation is noiseless, in simulation, and
confined to $N\in\{15,21\}$ by a measured compilation cliff rather than by
choice. And the modules are not equally supported: module C is not validated
as an independent intervention, and is retained only as part of the full
configuration.
